# AI & automation niche research: what works now, and why

Research date: 6 Oct 2026. Method: platform data (Meta, Emplifi), usage research (OpenAI/NBER), creator-ranking roundups (Favikon, SocialBook, 2026), creator profiles (n8n Creator Hub, creators' own sites), and a structural breakdown of the recurring formats these creators use.

> **Limit, stated plainly:** video platforms are blocked in this research environment, so no individual video was watched frame by frame. The analysis below covers formats and principles that are documented and repeated across many top creators. It does not claim view counts for specific videos. Use the "Swipe-file routine" at the end to keep checking this against live examples.

---

## 1. What the audience actually wants from AI content

The best predictor of useful AI content is what people really use AI for.

- OpenAI's large-scale study of ChatGPT use (NBER w34255, Sept 2025): **practical guidance (28.8%), seeking information (24.4%) and writing (23.9%) make up ~80% of conversations.** Most writing requests **edit, translate or critique the user's own text** rather than writing from scratch. **Over 70% of use is non-work.**
- Implication: the winning content is "help me do the thing I already do, better": emails, replies, CVs, studying, translating, fixing a sheet. Content about abstract AI futures or model benchmarks gets less traction.
- Bangladesh-specific fit: ~72M Facebook users, mostly 18–34, mostly male (NapoleonCat, Sep 2026). Hundreds of thousands of businesses sell through **Facebook Pages and Messenger** (F-commerce). The audience splits into (a) students and young professionals, (b) freelancers, (c) F-commerce and small-business owners. Group (c) is the n8n buyer.

## 3. Creator archetypes that win in this niche, and why

Each block covers the questions from the brief: attention, watch-through, rewatch, hook, clarity, visuals, relatability, why it beats weaker versions, and what we adapt.

### A. The no-hype news explainer (e.g. Rowan Cheung / The Rundown AI)
- **Why it gets attention:** fast, dated, specific ("X launched today"). It's novel and feels urgent without being clickbait.
- **Why people keep watching:** each item is short. A "number 3 of 3" structure creates a progress bar in the viewer's head.
- **Why the hook works:** it names the product and the change in the first sentence.
- **Why it's clear:** one sentence of what it is, one of what it means.
- **Why it beats weaker versions:** weak news content reads headlines aloud. Strong news content answers "so what for me?"
- **Adapt:** our news posts always end with *"what this means for you"* and *who can actually use it* (plan, country, price), because most news content skips this. We cap news at ~8% of the plan, since news ages fast.

### B. The practical AI teacher (e.g. Sabrina Ramonov; Jeff Su for productivity)
- **Attention:** a named, relatable outcome ("write emails that don't sound like AI").
- **Watch-through:** the steps show on screen as they're spoken. The viewer is *doing*, not just listening.
- **Rewatch / save:** contains a copyable prompt or setting that people want to keep.
- **Clarity:** one method per video. The full prompt goes in the caption.
- **Visuals:** talking head plus a screen recording, with zoom-ins on the exact button.
- **Why it beats weaker versions:** weak versions list 10 tools in 30 s and teach none.
- **Adapt:** one method per Reel, with the full prompt in the caption ("copy this"), and a 3-row or 1-minute test so the viewer sees proof that it works.

### C. The "build it with me" automation creator (e.g. Nate Herk, Nick Saraev; long-form YouTube, n8n)
- **Attention:** a business outcome ("an AI agent that answers my leads").
- **Watch-through:** the workflow canvas grows node by node, so visual progress pulls people along.
- **Rewatch:** viewers pause to copy node settings.
- **Clarity:** Nate Herk targets beginners with plain language. Nick Saraev goes deeper on architecture. Both explain *why* each node exists.
- **Why it beats weaker versions:** weak automation content shows a finished, tangled canvas with no story.
- **Adapt for Facebook Reels:** these creators work long-form, so we compress them. One workflow, 5–7 nodes, each node named in plain words ("this part checks for duplicates"), with a before/after result shot. The full build stays off Facebook. The Reel sells the *idea*, and the Messenger CTA sells the *build*.

### D. The business-AI translator (e.g. Allie K. Miller; Guilherme Karvatzki for small business)
- **Attention:** frames AI as a decision ("should you automate this?").
- **Why it works:** decision frameworks are highly saveable and shareable with a boss or partner.
- **Adapt:** decision trees, "automation math" cards and readiness checklists. These are our highest-save image formats.

### E. The short explainer (e.g. Tiffany Janzen)
- **Attention:** one concept, one analogy, under 40 s.
- **Rewatch:** short enough to loop, and the analogy rewards a second watch.
- **Adapt:** our "Explain It Simply" series covers webhook, API, AI agent, MCP and tokens, using **local analogies** (shop counter, doorbell, bank token line) instead of the overused "waiter" analogy.

## 5. What weaker AI content does wrong (our "never do" list)

1. **Tool dumps:** "10 AI tools you need". Nothing is taught and nothing is remembered.
2. **Hype hooks with no payoff:** "This AI will replace your job." It erodes trust, and Meta's interest survey punishes it.
3. **Outdated features:** e.g. still calling it NotebookLM after the July 2026 rename to Gemini Notebook, or promoting Gems after Google announced they start turning into skills on 17 November 2026.
4. **Narrating someone else's video.** This is now explicitly unoriginal on Facebook.
5. **Walls of text on images:** 150 words at 30 px. Nobody reads it on a phone.
6. **AI-sounding copy:** "unlock", "elevate", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world". Readers spot it instantly.
7. **CTA spam:** "comment YES", "tag 3 friends". It gets demoted, and it signals low value.
8. **Plans and prices left out:** a feature that's only on a $200/month plan or only in the US, presented as if everyone has it.

## 6. Principles we extracted (and apply in every script)

1. **Outcome before method.** The first line names what the viewer gets.
2. **One idea per post.** If it needs two ideas, it's two posts.
3. **Show, then tell.** Start the demo within 5 seconds.
4. **Proof beats claims.** Show a real result, a test, or a real workflow canvas.
5. **Specific beats general.** A named button, a real prompt, a real number of steps.
6. **Plain words, short sentences.** Written for people whose second language is English.
7. **A reason to save.** A prompt, a checklist, a setting path.
8. **An honest scope line.** Who can use it, and what it can't do.
9. **A loop when it fits.** The last line flows back into the first frame.
10. **The CTA matches the content.** "Save" for reference content, "message me" only where automation is the topic.

## 7. Swipe-file routine (keeps this research current, 20 min/week)

1. Every Monday, open Facebook Reels search and the Instagram Explore page for: *"ChatGPT tips", "AI automation", "n8n", "Gemini tips", "AI for business"*.
2. Save 5 Reels with high comments relative to likes. Comments show genuine usefulness.
3. For each, fill in the one-line grid: **hook | first visual change (sec) | the one idea | proof shown | CTA**.
4. Copy the *structure*, never the script, footage or audio.
5. Feed the grid into next month's plan (see `06-production/workflow.md`, Step 1).
