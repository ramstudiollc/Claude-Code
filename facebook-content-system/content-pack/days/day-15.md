## 09:30 · Image · D15-I1: Don't ask for a summary, ask for decisions

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Prompt card

- **The problem it solves:** Reading a long notice or letter and still not knowing what you're supposed to do.
- **The idea:** Asking AI for decisions, deadlines, amounts and consequences turns any document into an action list.
- **What they leave with:** Ask AI for the decisions, deadlines and consequences in a document instead of a summary.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save it for the next long document.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Don't ask for a summary. Ask for decisions.
- Body:
  - “Read this document: [paste].
  - 1. What decisions does it ask me to make?
  - 2. What are the deadlines and amounts?
  - 3. What happens if I do nothing?
  - 4. What should I ask before agreeing?”
- Footer: A summary tells you what it says. This tells you what to do.

**What the image shows:** Long document icon → four question chips → checklist.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Light background. Left: tall document icon with lines. Right: four numbered question chips. Footer band.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
“Summarise this” gives you a shorter document. You still don't know what to do.

Ask this instead:
“Read this document: [paste].
1. What decisions does it ask me to make?
2. What are the deadlines and amounts?
3. What happens if I do nothing?
4. What should I ask before agreeing?”

Works for school notices, bank letters, client briefs and policy updates.

Use it on the next bank letter, school notice or client brief that lands on you.
```

## 13:30 · Reel · D15-R1: Can't decide? Make AI build a weighted scorecard

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Prop demo + screen inserts · **Length:** 41 s

- **The problem it solves:** Going back and forth between two options for days without deciding.
- **The idea:** Give AI your priorities with weights and the real specs; get a scored table, then ask what would flip the result.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Stuck between two laptops for a week?
- **What the viewer wants to know:** Which one wins for my priorities, and why?
- **Where it pays off:** 22.5–37.5 s: weighted totals show the winner and exactly what would flip it.
- **What they leave with:** Give AI your priorities with weights and the real specs, then ask what would change the winner.
- **Call to action:** Question to the viewer: Ask what viewers are stuck choosing between.
- **Overall look:** Asif at a table with two real laptop boxes (props) → cut-in screen inserts: weights bar chart, scoring table with totals, 'what would flip it' answer → Asif picks up the winner.

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: A specific, relatable stall (a week stuck between two laptops) instead of a generic 'can't decide?'.
- Visual progression: Weights chart, then a scoring table, then highlighted totals.
- Usefulness: Works for any comparison: phones, laptops, couriers, job offers.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Stuck between two laptops (or phones, or job offers) for a week? Make AI build a weighted scorecard.

1. Your priorities, with weights: “Battery 40%, price 30%, weight 20%, ports 10%.”
2. Paste the real specs from the shop pages. Don't trust AI's memory for specs or prices.
3. “Score each option 1–10 on each priority, explain why, and give a weighted total.”
4. The smart question: “What would have to change for the other option to win?”

You still choose, but now you know exactly what you're trading.

What are you stuck choosing between right now?

#AItips #decisionmaking #productivity
```

## 18:30 · Image · D15-I2: Is it worth automating? The 1-minute math

**Series:** Automate This · **Content type:** Automation & n8n · **Format:** Framework card

- **The problem it solves:** A task feels like 'only a few minutes' until you see what it costs over a year.
- **The idea:** Minutes × frequency × 52 shows yearly time cost; compare with setup and running cost, plus mistake costs.
- **What they leave with:** Minutes per task × times per week × 52 shows the yearly cost of doing it by hand.
- **Call to action:** Question to the viewer: Ask what yearly number viewers got.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Is it worth automating? Do the 1-minute math
- Body:
  - Minutes per task × times per week × 52 = minutes per year
  - Example: 15 min × 10 times × 52 = 7,800 min = 130 hours a year
  - Compare with: setup time + monthly running cost
  - Add what mistakes cost you: lost orders, late replies
- Footer: If the yearly number scares you, it's worth a conversation.

**What the image shows:** Calculator-style card with the formula and a worked example.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Dark background. Formula in a large monospace-style block. Worked example underneath with the result '130 hours' in large accent numerals. Comparison lines below. Footer bottom.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Is a task worth automating? 1 minute of maths:

minutes per task × times per week × 52 = minutes per year

Example: 15 minutes × 10 times a week × 52 = 7,800 minutes = 130 hours a year.

Now compare that with the setup time and the monthly running cost. Then add what mistakes cost you: a lost order, a late reply, a wrong price.

If the yearly number scares you, it's worth a conversation.
```

## 21:00 · Reel · D15-R2: Human-in-the-loop: AI drafts, you tap Approve

**Series:** Automate This · **Content type:** Automation & n8n · **Format:** Screen demo + face cam · **Length:** 45 s

- **The problem it solves:** Wanting AI to help with customer replies but fearing it will send something wrong.
- **The idea:** An n8n workflow drafts replies with AI, then waits for a human Approve/Decline on Telegram before sending; n8n agents can also require approval before using tools.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** One wrong AI reply can cost a customer.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** How do you let AI help without letting it send on its own?
- **Where it pays off:** 7.5–21.5 s: the workflow pauses and asks on Telegram; Approve sends, Decline stops.
- **What they leave with:** Add a human Approve step so AI drafts customer replies but never sends them alone.
- **Call to action:** Question to the viewer: Ask whether viewers would trust AI replies without a check.
- **Overall look:** Phone showing a Telegram approval message with buttons → canvas with Gmail trigger, AI node and 'send and wait' node → both branches.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
One wrong AI reply can cost you a customer. So don't let AI send on its own. Add a human tap.

This n8n workflow:
1. A customer email arrives
2. AI drafts a reply
3. The workflow pauses and sends me the draft on Telegram with Approve / Decline buttons
4. Approve → it sends. Decline → nothing goes out.

Use it for anything that touches money, customers or your reputation. After a few weeks you'll see which replies are safe to automate fully.

Would you trust AI to reply to your customers without a check like this?

#n8n #AIautomation #humanintheloop
```
