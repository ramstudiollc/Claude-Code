## 13:30 · Reel · D19-R1: Before you sign anything, ask AI these 4 questions

- **What the viewer wants to know:** Which four questions?
- **Where it pays off:** 7.5–28 s: payments, one-sided rules, exit costs and unusual clauses, each with the exact quote.
- **What they leave with:** Before signing, ask AI about payments, one-sided rules, exit costs and unusual clauses, then read the quoted clause yourself.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save the four questions.
- **Overall look:** Long agreement on paper → marker redaction → AI answers with highlighted clauses.

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: Most people have signed something they didn't fully read; the question makes them admit it to themselves.
- Relatability: Rental agreements, job contracts and app terms are everyday documents.

- Novelty: 'What can they do that I can't?' is a sharp question most people never ask.
- Saves and shares: A list people keep for the next contract.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Signing something you haven't fully read? Rent, job, freelance, app terms: ask AI 4 questions first.

First, remove your ID numbers, signatures and account details.

1. “List every amount I pay, and every date.”
2. “What can they do that I can't?” (finds one-sided rules)
3. “How do I get out, and what does it cost me?”
4. “What's unusual compared with similar agreements? Quote the exact clause.”

Always read the quoted clause yourself.

Not legal advice. For big contracts, take these answers to a lawyer. You'll ask better questions and pay for less time.

Keep these for the next agreement someone hands you.

#AItips #contracts #ChatGPT
```

## 21:00 · Reel · D19-R2: AI detectors flag real human writing

**Series:** AI Myth Check · **Content type:** AI literacy & safety · **Format:** Green screen + talking head · **Length:** 42 s

- **The problem it solves:** Students being accused of using AI because a detector flagged their honest work.
- **The idea:** Studies and OpenAI's own experience show AI-text detectors are unreliable, especially for non-native English writers; a score is not proof.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** AI detectors flagged real student essays as AI.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** Why would a detector flag real writing?
- **Where it pays off:** 22.5–37 s: simple writing looks 'predictable'; a score is not proof; ask about drafts instead.
- **What they leave with:** AI-text detectors often flag real writing, especially by non-native English writers, so a score is not proof.
- **Call to action:** Share: Send it to a teacher.
- **Overall look:** Asif on green screen over the Patterns paper and the 2023 TechCrunch headline; simple stat cards.

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | AI detectors flagged real student essays as AI. | Real essays. Flagged as AI. | Asif on green screen; the Patterns paper title behind him (own capture). |
| 3-8 | Stanford researchers tested seven detectors on essays by non-native English speakers. | 7 detectors · 91 essays | Paper detail highlighted. |
| 8-12.5 | More than half were wrongly labelled as AI. | >50% wrongly flagged | Bar animation. |
| 12.5-17.5 | Even OpenAI shut down its own detector in 2023 for low accuracy. | OpenAI's own detector: shut down | Headline capture (TechCrunch, Jul 2023). |
| 17.5-22.5 | It caught only 26 percent of AI text, and flagged real writing too. | Caught 26% · flagged humans 9% | Stat cards. |
| 22.5-27 | Why? Simple, clear writing looks ‘predictable’, and that's what detectors look for. | Simple writing = 'looks AI' | Asif to camera. |
| 27-32 | So teachers and employers: a detector score is not proof. | A score is not proof | Card. |
| 32-37 | Ask about the work instead. Drafts, notes, a short conversation. | Ask about drafts + notes | Card. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: A surprising, unfair outcome (real essays flagged) creates instant concern.
- Novelty: Most people assume detectors work; OpenAI withdrawing its own is surprising.
- Relatability: Students in Bangladesh write in English as a second language, exactly the group the study found most affected.
- Payoff: Clear guidance for teachers and employers: a score is not proof.
- Saves and shares: Students will forward this to teachers, and teachers to colleagues.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
AI detectors labelled real student essays as AI-written.

• Stanford researchers ran 91 essays by non-native English writers through 7 popular detectors. More than half were wrongly flagged as AI. (Patterns, 2023)
• OpenAI shut down its own detector in July 2023 for “low accuracy”. It caught 26% of AI text and flagged 9% of human text as AI.

Why: simple, clear writing looks “predictable”, which is what detectors measure.

Teachers and employers: a detector score is not proof. Ask about drafts, notes, or have a short conversation about the work.

Send this to a teacher you know.

#AIliteracy #education #students
```
