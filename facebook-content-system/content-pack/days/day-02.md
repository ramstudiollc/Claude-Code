## 09:30 · Image · D02-I1: Prompt rescue: 6 follow-ups when the first answer is bad

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Prompt card

- **The problem it solves:** Getting a weak AI answer and wasting time starting a new chat.
- **The idea:** The second message fixes most bad answers; match the problem to a follow-up.
- **What they leave with:** Fix a bad AI answer with one targeted follow-up instead of starting over.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save it for the next disappointing answer.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Bad AI answer? Don't start over. Send one of these.
- Body:
  - Too generic → “Use my details: [paste facts].”
  - Too long → “Cut to 5 lines. Keep the numbers.”
  - Wrong tone → “Rewrite like I'm texting a respected client.”
  - Not sure it's true → “Which parts are you unsure about?”
  - Messy → “Make a table: step, owner, deadline.”
  - Missed the point → “My goal is [X]. What would you change?”

**What the image shows:** Two-column 'symptom → fix' card; symptoms in muted text, fixes as copyable quote chips.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Light background. Headline in 2 lines. Six rows: left label (muted, 36% width) → arrow → right quote chip (accent border). Series tag top-left.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Most people give up on a bad AI answer and open a new chat. The second message is usually where the good answer comes from.

Match the problem to the fix:
Too generic → “Use my details: [paste facts].”
Too long → “Cut to 5 lines. Keep the numbers.”
Wrong tone → “Rewrite like I'm texting a respected client.”
Not sure it's true → “Which parts are you unsure about?”
Messy → “Make a table: step, owner, deadline.”
```

## 13:30 · Reel · D02-R1: Point your camera at a problem with Gemini Live

**Series:** Hidden Button · **Content type:** Hidden features & tools · **Format:** Phone-in-hand demo · **Length:** 38 s

- **The problem it solves:** A device or paper you don't understand, and no idea what words to type into a search.
- **The idea:** Gemini Live's camera mode lets you show a real-world problem and talk through it; free on Android and iPhone.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Router blinking red? Don't type it. Show it.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** Can AI really read the router and know the fix?
- **Where it pays off:** 15–20 s: it reads the label, names the model and gives the first fix step.
- **What they leave with:** Use Gemini Live's free camera mode to show AI a real problem instead of describing it.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save it for the next thing that confuses you.
- **Overall look:** Over-the-shoulder phone shots of real objects (router, bill, form, error screen) with the live AI response.

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: Starts from a specific household moment (a blinking router) and flips the habit of typing problems.
- Novelty: Many viewers have never turned on the camera inside an AI chat; it feels like a hidden feature.
- Visual progression: Four different real objects in 12 seconds keep the visuals changing.
- Relatability: Router lights, confusing bills, English forms and error screens are daily pains in most homes.
- Usefulness: Exact taps (Gemini → Live → camera) and it's free, so viewers can try it immediately.
- Saves and shares: Easy to send to parents or friends who struggle with tech.

```text

#GeminiAI #AItips #techtips
```

**Notes:** Record the router demo for real and keep the actual answer. If Gemini gets it wrong, re-shoot with a clearer label view; never fake the response. Blur account numbers on the bill. Record vertical at 1080×1920 with phone screen capture + a second angle.

## 21:00 · Reel · D02-R2: Why AI makes things up (and the one prompt that catches it)

**Series:** AI Myth Check · **Content type:** AI literacy & safety · **Format:** Green screen + diagram · **Length:** 40 s

- **The problem it solves:** Trusting a confident AI answer and repeating something that turns out to be fake.
- **The idea:** AI predicts likely words, so it can produce realistic-looking fake sources; grounding + asking for exact quotes catches it.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** AI gave me a source. It doesn't exist.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** Why would AI invent a source that doesn't exist?
- **Where it pays off:** 3–16 s: it predicts what a source looks like; 25–35 s: the quote-the-line check catches it.
- **What they leave with:** AI predicts likely words, so ask it to quote the exact line behind every claim.
- **Call to action:** Follow: Follow for more AI Myth Checks.
- **Overall look:** Asif over a green-screen of a chat containing a fake citation; animated 'next-word' probability bars; 'NOT FOUND' stamp.

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | AI gave me a source. It doesn't exist. | A source that doesn't exist | Asif holding his phone: an AI answer with a neat book citation; quick cut to an empty search result. |
| 3-7 | It wasn't lying. It predicts the next likely word. | It predicts the next word | Animation: a sentence builds word by word with small probability bars. |
| 7-12.5 | Ask for a source it doesn't have, and it predicts what a source looks like. | predicts what a source LOOKS like | A citation assembles: author → title → year → page. |
| 12.5-16 | Real-looking author. Real-looking title. Doesn't exist. | looks real ≠ is real | Search result page → 'NOT FOUND' stamp. |
| 16-20 | That's a hallucination. Confident, fluent, and wrong. | Hallucination = confident + wrong | Label slides in; Asif points at it. |
| 20-25 | It happens most with exact numbers, dates, quotes, links and laws. | numbers · dates · quotes · links · laws | Five icons pop in sequence. |
| 25-30 | The fix: turn on web search, or give it your own document. | Fix: search ON or give it the source | Screen recording: toggling search; uploading a PDF. |
| 30-35 | Then ask: “Quote the exact line you used for each claim.” | “Quote the exact line for each claim.” | Typed prompt; answer shows quoted lines highlighted. |
| 35-40 | No quote, no trust. Follow for more AI myth checks. | No quote = no trust | Asif to camera; series tag 'AI Myth Check'. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: A personal, specific failure (a source that doesn't exist) that every AI user fears and wants explained.
- Curiosity: Viewers want to know why a tool would invent something; the reason arrives at second 3.
- Visual progression: A fake citation assembles piece by piece, then gets stamped NOT FOUND.
- Usefulness: Ends with a concrete check prompt viewers can use today.
- Novelty: Most people think AI 'searches' facts; learning it predicts words reframes every answer they've seen.
- Saves and shares: Strong 'send to a friend who trusts ChatGPT blindly' energy.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
AI gave me a perfect-looking source. It doesn't exist.

Language models generate the most likely next words. Ask for a source they don't have, and they can generate something that looks like a source: real-looking author, real-looking title, doesn't exist. That's a hallucination.

Highest risk: exact numbers, dates, quotes, links and laws.

The check I use:
1. Turn on web search, or upload the document yourself.
2. Ask: “Quote the exact line you used for each claim.”
No quote, no trust.

Follow for more AI Myth Checks.

#AIliteracy #ChatGPT #AItips
```

**Notes:** Create the fake-citation example yourself by asking an AI for 'a source' on an obscure claim with search off; record it. Show the real search result returning nothing. Don't name or shame a specific tool; the behaviour applies to all.
