## 09:30 · Image · D10-I1: Learn anything faster: ask for 3 levels of explanation

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Staircase card

- **The problem it solves:** Reading explanations that are either far too simple or far too advanced.
- **The idea:** Asking for child, student and expert levels shows exactly where your understanding stops.
- **What they leave with:** Ask for three levels of explanation and study the point where it starts to feel hard.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save it.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Confused by a topic? Ask AI for 3 levels
- Body:
  - “Explain [topic] at 3 levels:
  - 1. To a 10-year-old, in 3 sentences
  - 2. To a university student, with one example
  - 3. To an expert: what do beginners get wrong?”
  - Read level 1 first. Stop where it gets hard. That's your study point.
- Footer: Works for taxes, physics, marketing or n8n.

**What the image shows:** Three-step staircase: each step is one explanation level, with a flag where it gets hard.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Light background. Prompt block in the top half. Below, a three-step staircase (small → medium → tall), each step labelled with its level; a small flag on the step marked 'stop here = study here'. Footer bottom.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Confused by a topic? The fastest way I know to learn it with AI:

“Explain [topic] at 3 levels:
1. To a 10-year-old, in 3 sentences
2. To a university student, with one example
3. To an expert: what do beginners usually get wrong?”

Read level 1. Then level 2. The moment it gets hard is exactly where you should study.

Level 3 is the bonus: it shows the mistakes before you make them. Try it on the topic you've been avoiding.

#studytips #AItips #learning
```

## 21:00 · Reel · D10-R2: Meeting notes → action table (owner, deadline, MISSING)

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Workflows & productivity · **Format:** Before/after split screen · **Length:** 38 s

- **The problem it solves:** Leaving meetings unsure who is doing what, then arguing about it later.
- **The idea:** Paste a transcript or notes; ask for action/owner/deadline + open questions; force 'MISSING' instead of guesses.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Meetings end. Nobody knows who does what.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** What's the one line that stops AI guessing?
- **Where it pays off:** 13–28 s: 'write MISSING' turns gaps into a ready follow-up list.
- **What they leave with:** Ask AI to write MISSING instead of guessing owners or dates when it turns notes into tasks.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save it for your next meeting.
- **Overall look:** Closing laptop → split screen: left 'before' table with a guessed owner, right 'after' table with red MISSING cells → follow-up message.

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Meetings end. Nobody knows who does what. | Who does what? | Asif closes a laptop after a call; confused look. |
| 3-7.5 | Fix: paste the transcript or your rough notes into AI. | Paste transcript or notes | Screen: notes pasted. |
| 7.5-13 | Ask for a table: action, owner, deadline. Plus a list of open questions. | action · owner · deadline | Table appears. |
| 13-18 | Add this line, the most important one: “If it's missing, write MISSING.” | “Missing? Write MISSING.” | Line typed and highlighted. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: A universal workplace frustration stated in seven words.
- Relatability: Everyone has left a meeting unsure who owns what.
- Novelty: The 'write MISSING' rule is an unusual, specific trick most people haven't seen.
- Payoff: MISSING cells become a ready-made follow-up message.
- Saves and shares: Prompt in the caption; teams share it internally.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text

“From these notes, make a table: Action | Owner | Deadline. Then list open questions. Use only what's in the notes. If an owner or deadline is missing, write MISSING. Don't guess.”

Try it after your next meeting and count the MISSING cells.

#productivity #meetings #AItips
```
