## 09:30 · Image · D08-I1: Say this, not that: 4 prompt upgrades

**Series:** Before → After · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Before/after table

- **The problem it solves:** Getting vague AI answers because the request itself is vague.
- **The idea:** Weak prompts lack audience, limits and the user's own material; adding them improves answers a lot.
- **What they leave with:** Add who it's for, a limit and your own material to any prompt.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save it.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Say this, not that: 4 prompt upgrades
- Body:
  - “Write a caption” → “3 captions for [product], for mothers in Dhaka, under 40 words”
  - “Fix my CV” → “Rewrite these 3 bullets with results. Don't invent numbers.”
  - “Explain AI agents” → “Explain AI agents to a shop owner in 5 lines”
  - “Plan my week” → “Here are my 12 tasks and 3 deadlines. Plan Mon–Fri.”
- Footer: Better prompts add: audience, limit, your material.

**What the image shows:** Two-column table: greyed weak prompts left, bold strong prompts right.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Light background. Column headers 'Instead of' (grey) and 'Say' (accent). Four rows, left text grey 40%, right text dark 60%, arrow between. Footer band at bottom.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Same task, better prompt. 4 upgrades:

Instead of “Write a caption” → “Write 3 captions for [product], for mothers in Dhaka, under 40 words.”
Instead of “Fix my CV” → “Rewrite these 3 bullets to show results. Don't invent numbers.”
Instead of “Explain AI agents” → “Explain AI agents to a shop owner in 5 lines.”
```

## 18:30 · Image · D08-I2: See and delete what ChatGPT remembers about you

**Series:** Hidden Button · **Content type:** Hidden features & tools · **Format:** Hidden feature card

- **The problem it solves:** Not knowing what ChatGPT has quietly remembered about you.
- **The idea:** ChatGPT's memory is visible and editable; most users never check it.
- **What they leave with:** You can view, delete and switch off what ChatGPT remembers under Settings → Personalization → Memory.
- **Call to action:** Question to the viewer: Ask whether anything in their memory list surprised them.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: See what ChatGPT remembers about you
- Body:
  - Go to Settings → Personalization → Memory.
  - Open the saved list. Delete anything wrong or too personal.
  - Turn off 'reference chat history' if you don't want past chats used.
  - Or say in chat: “Forget that I…”, then check the list.
- Footer: Menu names can change between app versions. Look for Memory under Personalization.

**What the image shows:** Drawn settings path (breadcrumb style) + a list with a delete icon.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Light background. Breadcrumb path drawn as three rounded chips with arrows at the top. Below, a mock list of three memory items with trash icons. Instructions as numbered rows. Footer small.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
ChatGPT may remember things about you across chats. You can see exactly what, and delete it.

1. Settings → Personalization → Memory
2. Open the saved list and delete anything wrong or too personal
3. Turn off “reference chat history” if you don't want old chats used
```

## 21:00 · Reel · D08-R2: Tailor your CV to a job post without lying

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Workflows & productivity · **Format:** Talking head + screen split · **Length:** 46 s

- **The problem it solves:** Sending lots of job applications and hearing nothing back.
- **The idea:** Extract the job's requirements, match each to real evidence, rewrite matched bullets in the employer's words, and handle gaps honestly.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Thirty applications. Zero calls. Look at your CV.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** What's wrong with my CV, and how do I fix it without lying?
- **Where it pays off:** 22–38 s: the match table shows strengths and gaps; matched bullets are rewritten honestly in the employer's words.
- **What they leave with:** Match your CV to each job's own requirements and words, honestly, instead of sending one CV everywhere.
- **Call to action:** Share: Send to a friend who's job hunting.
- **Overall look:** Vertical split: Asif on the top half holding a printed CV, the screen on the bottom half: job post → requirement list → match table (green/red rows) → before/after bullet.
