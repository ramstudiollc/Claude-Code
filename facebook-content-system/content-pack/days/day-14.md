## 13:30 · Reel · D14-R1: Your AI might be living in the past (training cutoff)

**Series:** AI Myth Check · **Content type:** AI literacy & safety · **Format:** Green screen + diagram · **Length:** 30 s

- **The problem it solves:** Using an AI answer about prices, rules or 'the latest' that is quietly out of date.
- **The idea:** Models learn from data up to a cutoff; without web search they can state old information as current.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Today's price? AI might quote last year's.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** Why would AI give an old answer as if it's current?
- **Where it pays off:** 3–26.5 s: the training cutoff explained, then two checks (search on, dated sources).
- **What they leave with:** For anything recent, turn on web search and only trust answers that show dated sources.
- **Call to action:** Question to the viewer: Ask if viewers have caught AI giving outdated information.
- **Overall look:** Timeline with a 'cutoff' line; right side greyed; search toggle and dated sources.

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Today's price? AI might quote last year's. | Today's price? Last year's answer. | Asif on green screen; behind him a chat answer quoting an outdated price as 'current'. |
| 3-8 | Every AI model learns from data up to a certain date. Its cutoff. | Training cutoff | Timeline animation with a cutoff line. |
| 8-12.5 | After that date, it knows nothing, unless it searches the web. | After cutoff: blank | Right side of the timeline greys out. |
| 12.5-17 | Without search, it can confidently describe old prices as current. | Old info, stated as current | Example answer with an outdated price highlighted. |
| 17-22 | So for news, prices, laws or ‘the latest’ anything, check two things. | news · prices · laws · latest | Four icons. |
| 22-26.5 | Is web search on? And does the answer show sources with dates? | Search on? Dated sources? | Search toggle; source links with dates highlighted. |
| 26.5-30 | No dates, no trust. Check before you quote it. | No dates, no trust | Asif to camera; a dated source link highlighted. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: A concrete, costly mistake (quoting an old price) that makes viewers question answers they already trusted.
- Novelty: Many users don't know AI models have a training cutoff.
- Visual progression: A timeline animation with the cutoff line makes an invisible limit visible.
- Usefulness: Two quick checks viewers can apply to every time-sensitive answer.
- Payoff: Ends with a simple rule viewers can remember: no dates, no trust.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Ask AI for today's price and it might quote last year's.

Every AI model learns from data up to a cutoff date. After that, it knows nothing unless it searches the web, and without search it can describe old prices, laws or “the latest version” as if they're current.

For anything time-sensitive, check 2 things:
1. Is web search on?
2. Does the answer show sources with dates?

No dates, no trust.

Have you caught AI giving you outdated information?

#AIliteracy #ChatGPT #AItips
```

**Notes:** Generate a real outdated answer by asking a model with search off about a recent product; show it honestly. Don't single out one brand as 'bad'.

## 18:30 · Image · D14-I2: What's inside an AI agent: brain, instructions, memory, tools

**Series:** Explain It Simply · **Content type:** Automation & n8n · **Format:** Glossary card

- **The problem it solves:** Hearing 'AI agent' everywhere without knowing what's inside one.
- **The idea:** An agent = model + instructions + memory + tools; fewer tools mean fewer mistakes.
- **What they leave with:** An AI agent is a model plus instructions, memory and tools, and fewer tools means fewer mistakes.
- **Call to action:** No CTA (the takeaway is the ending): None. Ends with a practical question to ask anyone selling an 'AI agent'.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: What's inside an AI agent (4 parts)
- Body:
  - Brain: the AI model that reads and decides.
  - Instructions: the rules, written once (tone, limits, when to ask a human).
  - Memory: what it remembers about this customer or task.
  - Tools: what it may do. Read a sheet, send a message, book a slot.
- Footer: Fewer tools = fewer mistakes. Give an agent only what it needs.

**What the image shows:** Robot outline split into four labelled zones (head, notebook, chat bubble, toolbox).

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Dark background. Simple line-art agent figure in the centre with four callout lines to labelled boxes (brain/head, instructions/clipboard, memory/notebook, tools/toolbox). Footer bottom.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
“AI agent” sounds complicated. Inside, it's 4 parts:

🧠 Brain: the AI model that reads and decides
📋 Instructions: the rules, written once (tone, limits, when to hand over to a human)
🗂 Memory: what it remembers about this customer or task
🧰 Tools: what it's allowed to do (read a sheet, send a message, book a slot)

The rule I build by: fewer tools = fewer mistakes. Give an agent only what it needs for the job.

Next time someone sells you an “AI agent”, ask which tools it's allowed to use.

#AIagents #n8n #automation
```

**Notes:** Glossary/diagram hybrid. The four parts mirror n8n's AI Agent node (model, system message, memory, tools); you can show a small n8n reference in the caption reply if asked.

## 21:00 · Reel · D14-R2: Freelance proposals: let AI read the job post, you write the opener

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Workflows & productivity · **Format:** Before/after split screen · **Length:** 43 s

- **The problem it solves:** Sending proposals that get ignored because they open like everyone else's.
- **The idea:** Use AI to analyse the client's worries and words; write a 2-line opener yourself (their worry + your proof); let AI cut generic lines.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Clients read two lines. Then decide.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** What should the first two lines say instead?
- **Where it pays off:** 18–34 s: the client's worry becomes line one; proof becomes line two.
- **What they leave with:** Use AI to find the client's real worry, then open your proposal by naming it and showing proof.
- **Call to action:** Question to the viewer: Ask for the worst proposal opener viewers have seen.
- **Overall look:** Split screen: left = generic 'Dear Sir' proposal (crossed out), right = job-post analysis → typed two-line opener → AI edit suggestions; final full-width better proposal.

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|

| 7-12.5 | Use AI to read the job post first, not to write your proposal. | AI reads. You write. | Job post pasted into AI. |
