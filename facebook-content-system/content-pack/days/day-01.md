## 09:30 · Image · D01-I1: Automate, delegate or delete? A 3-question filter

**Series:** Automate This · **Content type:** Automation & n8n · **Format:** Decision tree

- **The problem it solves:** A to-do list full of repetitive tasks, and no idea which ones are worth automating.
- **The idea:** Before automating a task, check if it should exist and if its steps are fixed.
- **What they leave with:** Before automating a task, check that it should exist and that its steps are fixed.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save it and run your own to-do list through it.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Automate it, delegate it, or delete it?
- Subhead: Ask these 3 questions before you build anything.
- Body:
  - 1. Does this task need to happen at all? No → DELETE it.
  - 2. Are the steps the same every time? No → a person does it.
  - 3. Does it happen every week or more? Yes → AUTOMATE it.
- Footer: Automation makes a good process faster. It makes a bad process fail faster.

**What the image shows:** Clean vertical decision tree with three diamond questions and colour-coded exits.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Dark background. Headline top (2 lines). Three stacked diamond nodes down the centre; a 'No' arrow from each to a coloured tag on the right (red DELETE, amber PERSON, grey CHECKLIST for Q3 'No'); 'Yes' arrows go down; final green AUTOMATE pill at the bottom. Footer in small italic above the bottom margin.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Before you automate anything, ask if it should exist at all.

The 3-question filter I'd use before building any workflow:
1. Does this task need to happen? If not, delete it.
2. Are the steps the same every time? If not, a person should do it (or write the rules first).
3. Does it happen every week or more? Then automation pays back the setup time.

Automating a messy process just makes the mess faster.

Run your to-do list through it this week. You might delete a task before you automate one.

#automation #productivity #n8n
```

## 13:30 · Reel · D01-R1: Reverse prompting: make AI ask you questions first

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Talking head + screen split · **Length:** 34 s

- **The problem it solves:** You ask AI for help and get a bland, generic answer you can't actually use.
- **The idea:** Generic answers come from the AI guessing missing details; one line makes it ask instead of guess.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Your AI stays generic until you add this.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** What is the one line? It is held back until second 9.
- **Where it pays off:** 13–28 s: the AI asks five questions about your situation, and the second answer is visibly specific.
- **What they leave with:** Ask AI to question you before it answers, so it works from your facts instead of guesses.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save the line (it's in the caption).
- **Overall look:** Close-up talking head, cut to screen recording; side-by-side generic vs specific answer; final frame matches first frame for the loop.
- **Loop:** Last line 'Skip it, and…' runs straight into the opening 'your AI stays generic until you add this.'

## 18:30 · Image · D01-I2: 5 AI words explained in plain English

**Series:** AI Myth Check · **Content type:** AI literacy & safety · **Format:** Glossary card

- **The problem it solves:** AI news and tutorials full of words nobody explains.
- **The idea:** Most AI news is confusing because of five words; plain definitions fix that.
- **What they leave with:** Model, prompt, context, hallucination and agent are the five words that make most AI news understandable.
- **Call to action:** Share: Send it to someone who just started using AI.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: AI news confusing? Learn these 5 words first
- Body:
  - Model: the AI 'brain' (GPT, Gemini, Claude) that writes the answer.
  - Prompt: what you type or say to it.
  - Context: everything it can see right now: your chat, files, instructions.
  - Hallucination: a confident answer that is wrong or made up.
  - Agent: AI that can take actions (search, send, update), not only reply.
- Footer: Know these 5 and most AI news makes sense.

**What the image shows:** Dictionary-style card: five bold terms in the accent colour, one-line definitions.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Light background. Headline top. Five rows: term in bold accent colour (left, 30% width), definition (right, 70%). Thin divider lines. Footer bottom-left; series tag top-left.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
AI news confusing? Most of it depends on 5 words. Here they are in plain English.

Model: the AI 'brain' that writes the answer (GPT, Gemini, Claude are model families).
Prompt: what you type or say.
Context: everything the AI can see in this moment: your messages, files and instructions. More useful context, better answers.
Hallucination: a confident answer that's wrong.
Agent: AI that can do things (search, send, update a sheet) instead of only replying.

Know someone who just started using AI? This one's for them.
```
