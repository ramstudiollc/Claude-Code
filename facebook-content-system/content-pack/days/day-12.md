## 09:30 · Image · D12-I1: Myth: 'Act as an expert' makes AI smarter

**Series:** AI Myth Check · **Content type:** AI literacy & safety · **Format:** Myth vs fact

- **The problem it solves:** Copying 'Act as an expert' prompts and still getting average answers.
- **The idea:** A role changes tone, not knowledge; facts, goal, example and format do the heavy lifting.
- **What they leave with:** A role changes the tone, not the knowledge, so give AI your facts, goal, an example and a format.
- **Call to action:** No CTA (the takeaway is the ending): None. 'Context beats costume' closes it.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Myth: “Act as an expert” makes AI smarter
- Body:
  - FACT: A role sets the tone. It doesn't add knowledge.
  - What helps: your facts, your goal, an example, a format.
  - Weak: “Act as a marketing expert. Write my ad.”
  - Strong: “Product, price, buyer and offer are… Write 3 ads under 40 words.”
- Footer: Context beats costume.

**What the image shows:** Costume illustration (a hat labelled 'expert') crossed out vs a toolbox labelled 'context'.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Top: MYTH tag + headline with the quoted phrase struck through. Middle: FACT rows. Bottom: weak (grey) vs strong (accent) prompt blocks side by side. Footer as a short bold line.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
“Act as an expert marketer…” is one of the most copied prompt openers online. It mostly changes the tone, not the quality.

What actually improves answers:
• your facts (product, price, buyer)
• your goal (what should the reader do?)
• an example of what good looks like
• a format (3 options, under 40 words)

Weak: “Act as a marketing expert. Write my ad.”
Strong: “My product is…, price…, buyer…, offer… Write 3 ads under 40 words.”

Context beats costume.

#AItips #PromptTips #ChatGPT
```

**Notes:** Myth-vs-fact template with the 'costume vs toolbox' doodle. Keep the claim modest: roles affect tone/focus, they don't add facts.

## 13:30 · Reel · D12-R1: Better product photos with AI, without lying to customers

**Series:** Before → After · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Before/after split screen · **Length:** 42 s

- **The problem it solves:** Good products look cheap in phone photos taken at home.
- **The idea:** Ask AI to change only background and light, keep the product identical, verify details, and post one untouched photo too.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Looks great in hand. Cheap in photos?
- **What the viewer wants to know:** Can AI make it look better without changing the product?
- **Where it pays off:** 17–37 s: a clean result, a zoomed check of label and colour, and one untouched photo posted too.
- **What they leave with:** Ask AI to change only the background and light, then check the label, colour and edges at full zoom.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save the prompt.
- **Overall look:** Split: phone photo of a mug on a messy table → clean studio-style shot; zoomed comparison of the label.

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Looks great in hand. Cheap in photos? | Great in hand. Cheap in photos? | Split screen: messy phone photo of a mug (left) → clean studio shot (right). |
| 3-7 | Here's a normal phone photo. Messy table, yellow light. | Before: messy + yellow | Zoom on the before photo. |
| 7-12.5 | I upload it and say: keep the product exactly the same. Shape, colour, label, size. | Keep the product EXACTLY the same | Screen: prompt typed in ChatGPT or Gemini image editing. |
| 12.5-17 | Only change the background and the light. | Change only: background + light | Prompt continues. |
| 17-21.5 | Here's the result. Now the most important step. | After | Result reveal with a soft flash. |
| 21.5-27 | Zoom in. Compare the label, the colour, the edges. AI sometimes changes small details. | Check: label · colour · edges | Side-by-side zoom on the label; any changed detail circled. |
| 27-32 | If the product changed, don't use it. Customers notice. | Product changed? Don't post it | Red X over a version with an altered label. |
| 32-37 | And post one untouched photo too. Clean, but honest. | Post 1 real photo too | Post mock: edited photo + original photo side by side. |
| 37-42 | Clean photos, real product. That's the rule. | Clean photo · real product | End card. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: Every seller knows the gap between how a product looks in hand and in a phone photo.
- Relatability: Most Facebook sellers shoot products on phones in home light.
- Visual progression: Clear before → after reveal, then a zoomed side-by-side check.
- Payoff: A clean, sellable photo that still shows the real product.
- Usefulness: Exact prompt + a 3-point verification habit.
- Saves and shares: Sellers forward this to partners and staff who post products.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Looks great in your hand, cheap in your photos? Fix it with AI, without misleading customers.

Prompt (works in ChatGPT or Gemini image editing):
“Keep the product exactly the same: shape, colour, label, size and texture. Only replace the background with a plain light-grey studio background and soft natural shadow. Fix the lighting to neutral daylight.”

Then check 3 things at full zoom: label text, colour, edges. AI sometimes changes small details. If the product changed, don't use it.

And post one untouched photo too. Clean, but honest.

Try it on your next product, and post the untouched photo too.

#fcommerce #productphotography #AItips
```

**Notes:** Use a real product you own (mug, bag). Generate the edit live and show the genuine result, including any detail it got wrong. That honesty is the point of the Reel. Don't fabricate a perfect result.

## 21:00 · Reel · D12-R2: What is an API? Think of a shop counter

**Series:** Explain It Simply · **Content type:** Automation & n8n · **Format:** Diagram explainer + talking head · **Length:** 30 s

- **The problem it solves:** Hearing 'API' constantly and not knowing what it means for the tools you use.
- **The idea:** An API is a counter with a fixed menu: your app asks for menu items with its key and gets a reply; off-menu requests get errors.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** An API is just a shop counter.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** How is software like a shop counter?
- **Where it pays off:** 11–24 s: apps ask for 'menu items' with a key card; off-menu requests get errors.
- **What they leave with:** An API is a fixed menu one app offers another, and the API key is the card that proves who's asking.
- **Call to action:** Question to the viewer: Ask which tech word to explain next.
- **Overall look:** Illustrated shop counter with a menu board; two app icons exchanging a request and a reply; key card.
- **Loop:** Ends '…picture' over the counter; the opening line completes it: 'An API is a shop counter.'

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | An API is just a shop counter. | API = shop counter | Asif next to an illustrated shop counter. |
| 3-7 | You don't walk into the warehouse. You ask at the counter. | Don't enter the warehouse | Closed warehouse door; customer at the counter window. |
| 7-11 | And you can only ask for what's on the menu. | Only what's on the menu | Menu board lights up. |
| 11-15.5 | Apps work the same. Your app asks Google Sheets: add this row. | App → “add this row” | Two app icons; request arrow travels. |
| 15.5-19.5 | Sheets checks your card, your API key, and does it. | API key = your member card | Key card scan; green light. |
| 19.5-24 | Ask for something not on the menu, and you get an error. | Not on the menu → error | Red error code bubble. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: An unexpected comparison between tech jargon and a familiar shop.
- Curiosity: Viewers wait to see how a counter maps to software.
- Novelty: A fresh analogy instead of the overused 'waiter' explanation.
- Rewatch: The ending loops into the opening line.
- Usefulness: Teaches the practical rule 'never share your API key'.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
What is an API? Think of a shop counter.

You don't walk into the warehouse. You ask at the counter, and only for what's on the menu.

Apps work the same way: your app asks Google Sheets' “counter” to add a row. Sheets checks your card (your API key) and does it. Ask for something that's not on the menu → error.

That's also why you never share an API key. It's your card.

Which tech word should I explain next?

#API #automation #n8n
```
