## 13:30 · Reel · D07-R1: 5 signs a task should be automated

**Series:** Automate This · **Content type:** Automation & n8n · **Format:** Numbered list + talking head · **Length:** 43 s

- **The problem it solves:** Spending part of every day copying information from one app to another.
- **The idea:** Same steps, copy-paste, frequency, costly mistakes and someone waiting = automate; judgment-heavy = keep human.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Copy-pasting daily? That's a machine's job.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** Does my task pass the five signs?
- **Where it pays off:** 28–38 s: three or more signs means automate; needs judgment means keep it human.
- **What they leave with:** A task with fixed steps, copy-paste, high frequency, costly mistakes or someone waiting is worth automating.
- **Call to action:** Question to the viewer: Comment which task scored highest.
- **Overall look:** Counter graphic 1–5 with icons; tally at the end; 'keep it human' card.

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Copy-pasting daily? That's a machine's job. | Copy-paste daily = machine's job | Asif copy-pasting between two windows, then stops and looks at camera. |
| 3-5.5 | Five signs to check. | 5 signs | Counter graphic appears. |
| 5.5-10 | One: the steps are the same every time. Same apps, same order. | 1 · Same steps | Icon 1 with repeating arrows. |
| 10-14.5 | Two: you copy-paste between apps. Messenger to sheet, sheet to email. | 2 · Copy-paste between apps | Animated cursor copy-pasting between two windows. |
| 14.5-18.5 | Three: it happens every day or every week. | 3 · Daily or weekly | Calendar pages flip. |
| 18.5-23.5 | Four: a small mistake costs you. A wrong price, a missed order. | 4 · Mistakes cost money | Red alert icon. |
| 23.5-28 | Five: someone is waiting. A customer, a boss, a delivery man. | 5 · Someone is waiting | Clock ticking icon. |
| 28-33.5 | Three or more signs? That task is a strong candidate. | 3+ signs = automate | Tally marks animate 1-2-3. |
| 33.5-38 | But if it needs your judgment every single time, keep it human. | Needs judgment? Keep it human. | Asif to camera. |
| 38-43 | Which task scored highest for you? Tell me below. | Your task? ↓ | End card. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: Calls out a daily chore viewers do and reframes it as a machine's job, which is mildly provocative and relatable.
- Relatability: Copy-pasting from Messenger to a sheet and waiting customers are daily realities for sellers.
- Usefulness: Viewers can score their own tasks right away (3+ = automate).
- Visual progression: A new numbered icon every ~4.5 s; tally animation at the end.
- Payoff: The 'keep it human' exception adds an honest twist that builds trust.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Copy-pasting the same thing every day? That's a machine's job. 5 signs a task should be automated:

1. The steps are the same every time
2. You copy-paste between apps (Messenger → sheet → email)
3. It happens daily or weekly
4. A small mistake costs money (wrong price, missed order)
5. Someone is waiting (customer, boss, delivery)

3 or more? Strong candidate.
Needs your judgment every time? Keep it human. Automation handles rules; decisions stay with you.

Which task scored highest for you?

#automation #smallbusiness #n8n
```

**Notes:** List template with large numerals. Reply to comments with a one-line 'trigger → steps → result' for their task (consistent with D01-R2).

## 21:00 · Reel · D07-R2: Write emails that don't sound like AI

**Series:** Before → After · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Before/after split screen · **Length:** 40 s

- **The problem it solves:** Sending AI-written emails that sound stiff and fake to the people reading them.
- **The idea:** AI fills space with safe, long, polite filler; notes + your own sample + hard limits fix it.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** People can tell your email came from AI.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** What gives it away, and how do you fix it?
- **Where it pays off:** 26–36.5 s: a half-length rewrite that sounds human, plus the read-aloud test.
- **What they leave with:** Give AI your notes, one real email you wrote and a word limit, then read the result aloud.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save the prompt.
- **Overall look:** Generic email with filler highlighted in yellow → rewritten short email; split before/after.

**Video script** (times in seconds; say the voiceover word for word)

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | People can tell your email came from AI. | They can tell. | Asif reads a long generic email, winces. |
| 3-7.5 | “I hope this email finds you well.” Nobody talks like that. | “I hope this finds you well…” | Phrase highlighted in yellow. |
| 7.5-12 | AI fills empty space with polite, safe, long sentences. | polite · safe · long | More filler phrases highlight one by one. |
| 12-17 | Fix it with three rules. One: give it your rough notes, not a topic. | 1 · Your notes, not a topic | Bullet notes typed. |
| 17-21.5 | Two: paste one real email you wrote, so it copies your voice. | 2 · One email you wrote | Sample email pasted. |
| 21.5-26 | Three: set limits. Under 100 words. No opening small talk. | 3 · Under 100 words | Limits typed. |
| 26-31.5 | Before and after. Same message, half the length, and it sounds like a person. | Before → After | Split screen: long generic email vs short clear one. |
| 31.5-36.5 | Last step: read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it, delete it. | Read it out loud | Asif mouthing the email. |
| 36.5-40 | The prompt is in the caption. Save it. | Prompt ↓ Save it | End card. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: Calls out something people suspect about their own emails, which creates mild discomfort and curiosity.
- Relatability: 'I hope this email finds you well' is instantly recognised.
- Problem → solution: Three numbered rules, each shown on screen, then the fixed email.
- Payoff: Before/after: half the length and clearly human.
- Saves and shares: The prompt in the caption is reusable for every email.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
People can tell when an email came from AI. It fills empty space with safe, polite, long sentences.

Fix it with 3 rules:
1. Give it your rough notes, not a topic
2. Paste one real email you wrote, so it copies your voice
3. Set limits: under 100 words, no opening small talk

Prompt:
“Write an email from these notes: [notes]. Match the style of this email I wrote: [paste]. Under 100 words. No ‘I hope this finds you well’. Plain, direct, friendly.”

Then read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it, delete it. The prompt works for every email, so keep it handy.

#emailtips #AItips #productivity
```

**Notes:** Use a realistic but fictional email (supplier delay). Generate the 'before' with a bare prompt and the 'after' with the caption prompt, live.
