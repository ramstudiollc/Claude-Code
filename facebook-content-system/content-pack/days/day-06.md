## 09:30 · Image · D06-I1: Myth: you need to code to automate

**Series:** AI Myth Check · **Content type:** Automation & n8n · **Format:** Myth vs fact

- **The problem it solves:** Believing automation is only for programmers.
- **The idea:** Visual tools make automation about process thinking, not code; AI now writes the small code bits.
- **What they leave with:** If you can write a process down step by step, you can learn to automate it with visual tools.
- **Call to action:** No CTA (the takeaway is the ending): None. The closing line is the takeaway.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Myth: you need to code to automate
- Body:
  - FACT: Tools like n8n, Make and Zapier are visual. You connect boxes.
  - You need: a clear process, the apps' logins, and patience to test.
  - Code helps for edge cases. AI can now write those small bits.
  - The real skill is thinking in steps: trigger → steps → result.
- Footer: Can you write the steps on paper? You can learn to automate them.

**What the image shows:** Myth (crossed out) vs Fact panels; small node-graph illustration.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Top band red-tinted: 'MYTH' tag + headline with strike-through on 'you need to code'. Lower area green-tinted: 'FACT' rows. Small node-graph doodle bottom-right.

```text
Myth: you need to be a programmer to automate your work.

Fact: n8n, Make and Zapier are visual tools. You connect boxes: “when this happens → do this → then this”.

What you actually need:
• a clear process (write it on paper first)
```

## 13:30 · Reel · D06-R1: Brain-dump by voice, get a clean plan

**Series:** Before → After · **Content type:** Workflows & productivity · **Format:** Phone-in-hand demo · **Length:** 36 s

- **The problem it solves:** Too many tasks in your head to even decide where to begin.
- **The idea:** Speaking a 2-minute messy brain-dump into voice mode, then asking for a grouped plan, beats typing.
- **Hook (first 3 s):** Head too full to start? Talk it out.
- **What the viewer wants to know:** Can a messy ramble really turn into a plan?
- **Where it pays off:** 17–27 s: a grouped plan appears, including a task mentioned only once in passing.
- **What they leave with:** Talk through everything in voice mode, then ask AI to turn it into a grouped plan with next steps.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save the prompt from the caption.
- **Overall look:** Asif walking and talking into the phone; waveform; messy transcript turns into a neat grouped plan.

| Time | Voiceover (say) | On-screen text | What to show |

| 13-17 | Then one sentence: “Turn this into a plan.” | “Turn this into a plan.” | Large text on screen. |
| 17-22.5 | Add: “Group by project, give each task a next step, flag anything urgent.” | group · next step · urgent | Screen: structured plan appears. |
| 22.5-27 | Look. Things I mentioned once, now in the right place. | Nothing lost | Highlight a task mentioned in passing, now under the right project. |

**Why people keep watching**
- Hook: Names the overwhelm of a full head and offers a low-effort start: just talk.
- Relatability: A full head and scattered tasks is a feeling everyone knows.
- Visual progression: Messy transcript visibly reorganises into grouped tasks.
- Payoff: A task mentioned once in passing shows up in the right group: proof it works.
- Usefulness: Free voice mode in common AI apps plus a one-line prompt; works in Bangla or English.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Head too full to even start? Don't type. Talk.

1. Open voice mode in your AI app (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude all have one)
2. Talk for 2 minutes: half-sentences, worries, random tasks. Bangla, English, both.
3. Then say: “Turn this into a plan. Group by project, give each task one next step, and flag anything urgent or due this week.”

Faster than typing, and nothing you mentioned gets lost.

Try it tonight when your head is full.

#productivity #AItips #voiceAI
```

**Notes:** Record a genuine brain-dump (real tasks, nothing private). Show the actual transcript. Speed-ramp 2 minutes down to ~5 s.

## 18:30 · Image · D06-I2: Angry customer? Compare a defensive reply with a calm one

**Series:** Steal This Prompt · **Content type:** Practical AI · **Format:** Before/after chat mock

- **The problem it solves:** An angry customer message arrives and the first reply that comes to mind is defensive.
- **The idea:** A structured prompt produces a calm, human reply with options instead of excuses.
- **What they leave with:** A calm reply says sorry once, repeats the problem and offers two clear options.
- **Call to action:** Save: Save it for your next difficult message.

**Text on the image** (use exactly)

- Headline: Angry customer? Compare these two replies
- Body:
  - Customer: “3 days late and no update. Very bad service.”
  - ✗ “Sorry for the inconvenience. Delays happen due to courier issues.”
  - ✓ “You're right, 3 days with no update isn't OK. I'm sorry. I can resend today or refund now. Which do you prefer?”
  - Prompt: “Reply: sorry once, repeat their problem, 2 options, under 80 words.”
- Footer: Check names, prices and promises before you send.

**What the image shows:** Phone chat mock: customer complaint, a red-outlined defensive reply, a green-outlined calm reply, and the prompt strip.

**Layout idea** (your brand guidelines decide colours and fonts): Phone chat mock: grey customer bubble on top; red-outlined defensive reply; green-outlined calm reply; prompt strip at the bottom. Footer below the phone.

**Caption** (copy and paste)

```text
Angry customer message? The first reply that comes to mind is usually defensive.

✗ “Sorry for the inconvenience. Delays happen due to courier issues.”
✓ “You're right, 3 days with no update isn't OK. I'm sorry. I can resend today or refund now. Which do you prefer?”

The second one says sorry once, shows you understood, and gives a choice. AI can draft it for you:
“Here's a customer message: [paste]. Write a reply that says sorry once without excuses, repeats their problem in one line, offers 2 clear options with times, and sounds like a calm human. Under 80 words.”

Check every name, price and promise before sending. Keep the prompt for the next difficult message.

#customerservice #AItips #smallbusiness
```
